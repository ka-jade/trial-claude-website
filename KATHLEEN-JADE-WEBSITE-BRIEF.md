# Kathleen Jade Website --- Design, Content & Implementation Brief

**Document status:** Authoritative project brief\
**Project:** Kathleen Jade's Website\
**Site owner:** Kathleen Jade, ND\
**Repository:** `ka-jade/trial-claude-website`\
**Deployment:** Cloudflare Pages

## 1. Purpose

Kathleen Jade's website is a personal research and ideas site focused on
the biology of health and the evidence surrounding interventions
intended to influence it.

It is **not primarily a clinical-practice website, supplement
storefront, or conventional wellness website**.

The site should provide thoughtful, evidence-based interpretation of
scientific and clinical research in areas including systems medicine;
genomics and multiomics; microbiome science; nutrition and lifestyle
interventions; botanical medicine and dietary supplements; pharmacology
and therapeutics; longevity and aging; precision and personalized
health; digital health technologies; and emerging approaches such as
digital twins and AI-enabled biomedical research.

The site should become a place readers can return to for rigorous
reviews of significant research and emerging developments.

The goal is not to advocate for natural, pharmaceutical, technological,
or conventional approaches as categories. Evaluate interventions
according to the evidence.

## 2. Author and intellectual position

Kathleen Jade, ND, is a former clinician with training and experience
spanning naturopathic medicine, functional medicine, genomics, precision
health, nutrition, dietary supplements, pharmaceuticals, and scientific
literature evaluation.

Her principal strength is translating scientific and clinical research
into practical understanding without overstating what the evidence
demonstrates.

The website should reflect **scientific curiosity + critical
evaluation + clinical perspective + systems thinking**.

Kathleen should come across as someone who is interested in promising
ideas but is difficult to impress without evidence. Positive, negative,
null, conflicting, and uncertain findings are all relevant.

## 3. Audience

The site should be sophisticated enough to be credible to scientifically
literate readers while remaining accessible to educated nonspecialists.

Potential readers include clinicians and healthcare professionals;
researchers and scientists; biotechnology and health-technology
professionals; scientifically engaged consumers; and people interested
in evidence-based approaches to health and longevity.

Do not assume that readers are either patients seeking treatment or
academics reading a journal article.

# VISUAL SYSTEM

## 4. Core visual character

The site should feel **quietly sophisticated, intellectually curious,
organic, contemporary, scientific without being clinical, and artistic
without being decorative**.

The site should not resemble a typical naturopathic,
functional-medicine, supplement, spa, or wellness website. It should
also avoid the opposite cliché: glossy futuristic biotech imagery.

The visual identity should occupy the territory between **natural
history + contemporary biology + editorial design + systems science**.

## 5. Central visual idea: organic intelligence

The underlying metaphor is **organic intelligence**.

Biology is represented as an interconnected system operating across
scales:

**DNA → molecules → networks → cells → tissues → organisms → behavior →
environment**

Scientific and natural forms may therefore transform into, branch from,
dissolve into, or visually echo one another.

The purpose is not surrealism for its own sake. The transformations
should suggest that apparently different biological structures
participate in interconnected systems.

## 6. Artwork language

Artwork throughout the site should form a recognizable **visual
vocabulary**, not a collection of unrelated illustrations.

Core characteristics:

-   watercolor
-   translucent layering
-   soft and partially disappearing edges
-   substantial negative space
-   asymmetric composition
-   edge-weighted or partially cropped biological forms
-   organic structures merging, branching or dissolving into one another
-   recognizable scientific structures without textbook literalism
-   scientific information embedded within organic form
-   more variation in transparency than saturation
-   visual complexity concentrated selectively rather than distributed
    everywhere

Artwork should often feel as though it is **emerging from or
disappearing into the page**.

### Preferred palette

Primary territory: deep indigo, navy, mineral blue, blue-gray.

Secondary territory: lavender, muted mauve/aubergine, restrained
biological neutrals.

Sparse accents: ochre, gold, orange.

Backgrounds should generally be warm paper/off-white rather than stark
white.

Warm accent colors should remain subordinate. Avoid allowing the work to
become predominantly rusty brown, orange, pink, or conventionally
"pretty."

### Avoid

Do not introduce generic wellness imagery, spa aesthetics, decorative
botanical wallpaper, stock medical imagery, literal organ illustrations
merely to signal "health," glossy 3D molecules, generic futuristic
biotech graphics, excessive gradients or visual effects, maximalism,
excessive color, dense decorative pattern, obvious AI-generated visual
spectacle, or unnecessary scientific motifs added merely to make an
image appear scientific.

## 7. Scientific morphology rule

This is one of the most important principles in the visual system:

> **The scientific discipline determines the structural grammar; the
> Kathleen Jade visual language determines how that structure is
> rendered.**

Artwork for different research areas should therefore not simply swap
one icon for another. Instead, examine the actual visual structures
associated with the science.

Examples:

-   **Systems medicine / multiomics:** networks, nodes, modules, hubs,
    bridging relationships, variable density.
-   **Genomics:** sequence, helices, chromosomal organization,
    regulatory structure, variation.
-   **Microbiome:** ecological populations, communities, gradients,
    colonies, interaction networks.
-   **Neuroscience:** branching morphology, dendritic structures,
    connectivity.
-   **Botanical medicine:** plant morphology, vascular structures,
    phytochemical relationships.

These structures may then be interpreted through the site's watercolor,
translucent, asymmetric visual language.

Scientific visualization conventions may inspire artwork, but conceptual
artwork must **not falsely imply that it represents an actual dataset**.

Do not invent gene names, protein labels, quantitative axes,
experimental values, or other elements that could make conceptual
artwork look like scientific evidence.

## 8. The octopus

The octopus is a **signature motif**, not a logo and not a mandatory
element. It represents organic intelligence, distributed biological
organization, adaptability, and interconnectedness. Its effectiveness
depends partly on rarity.

When used, favor large cropped or asymmetric forms; allow substantial
portions to extend beyond the frame; use translucency; soften or
dissolve edges; permit tentacles to transition into other biological
structures; and maintain some recognizability without requiring literal
realism.

Possible transformations include **tentacle → DNA**, **DNA → botanical
stem**, **leaf venation → vascular branching**, and **vascular branching
→ neuronal morphology**.

Avoid dark marine realism, prominently rendered suckers, literal
underwater scenes, and using an octopus on every page.

The broader visual identity must remain recognizable **without the
octopus**.

# TYPOGRAPHY & COMPOSITION

## 9. Typography

Use typography to distinguish **ideas** from **information**.

Use a high-contrast editorial serif primarily for major statements, page
titles, article titles, and selected conceptual headings.

Use an extremely clean sans serif for navigation, explanatory copy,
metadata, captions, labels, and supporting information.

### Current working fonts

-   **Newsreader** --- display/editorial serif
-   **Instrument Sans** --- sans serif

These are **provisional**, not immutable brand fonts. Do not change them
casually. Any future font change should be evaluated across the whole
visual system rather than page by page.

## 10. Space is structural

Generous whitespace is a defining feature of the website.

Do not tighten layouts merely to fit more above the fold, fill an
apparently empty area, display more content simultaneously, or make a
page look busier or more conventionally "designed."

Empty space creates hierarchy and allows complex biological artwork to
remain sophisticated rather than decorative.

The site should feel edited, not filled.

## 11. Editorial rather than card-based design

Do **not default to card grids**.

Research content should generally behave more like an editorial
publication or research index than an app dashboard.

Prefer typography, hierarchy, thin rules, spacing, sequential reading,
and restrained metadata before introducing cards, boxes, shadows,
thumbnails, badges, icons, or decorative containers.

A component should exist because it improves information architecture,
not because an empty area appears to need something.

# INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

## 12. Current principal research areas

The current homepage architecture identifies:

1.  **Systems Medicine**
2.  **Microbiome**
3.  **Diet and Lifestyle Therapies**
4.  **Supplements**
5.  **Longevity**

These categories are **current working architecture**, not permanently
fixed taxonomy. They may evolve as substantive content accumulates.

Do not add categories merely to make the website appear more complete.

## 13. Research-area pages

Research-area landing pages should establish the intellectual territory
of a field rather than behave like generic category archives.

A useful general structure is:

**research-area identity → concise conceptual introduction → subject
artwork → substantial transition space → Research Reviews index**

Do not mechanically reproduce an identical template if the scientific
subject benefits from a different composition.

Consistency should come primarily from the design system, not rigid page
duplication.

# RESEARCH REVIEWS

## 14. Review index

Research Reviews should generally use a **single vertical editorial
index**.

Default behavior: article title, concise description/dek when useful,
restrained metadata, thin rules where useful, and generous vertical
spacing.

Do not use thumbnails by default. Do not convert the index into a
three-column card grid. Do not invent article titles or content merely
to make a layout appear populated.

Placeholder content must be explicitly identified as placeholder content
and removed before publication.

# CONTENT & EVIDENCE

## 15. Scientific standards

Scientific credibility is central to the website.

When producing research content:

-   prioritize primary literature when feasible
-   use systematic reviews and meta-analyses appropriately
-   distinguish observational evidence from randomized trials
-   distinguish mechanistic plausibility from demonstrated clinical
    benefit
-   identify important limitations
-   report null and negative findings
-   acknowledge meaningful conflicting evidence
-   avoid causal language when the evidence is associative
-   distinguish surrogate endpoints from clinically meaningful outcomes
-   distinguish statistical significance from clinical importance
-   avoid extrapolating animal or in-vitro findings directly to humans

Where appropriate, consider study population, sample size, intervention
and comparator, duration, outcome measures, effect magnitude, confidence
intervals, confounding, risk of bias, generalizability,
funding/conflicts of interest, replication, and biological plausibility.

Do not manufacture certainty where the literature does not support it.

## 16. Voice

The site's written voice should be **measured, precise, curious,
evidence-based, readable, and intellectually confident without being
dogmatic**.

Avoid wellness marketing, breathless claims, fear-based health language,
clickbait, exaggerated certainty, indiscriminate enthusiasm for
"natural" treatments, indiscriminate dismissal of conventional medicine,
unnecessary academic jargon, and simplistic consumer-health prose.

Readers should feel that the author is willing to say **this looks
promising**, **this effect appears clinically meaningful**, **the
mechanism is interesting but clinical evidence is weak**, **the trial
did not show benefit**, or **we do not know yet**, depending on what the
evidence supports.

# IMPLEMENTATION PRINCIPLES

## 17. Source of truth

The GitHub repository is the canonical source for website code.

Before implementing changes:

1.  inspect the current repository state
2.  understand existing HTML/CSS/components
3.  preserve working design-system conventions
4.  distinguish intentional design decisions from bugs
5.  avoid unnecessary refactoring

Do not redesign an existing component merely because another
implementation is possible.

## 18. Current technical character

The site is intentionally a relatively simple static website.

Do not introduce a framework, CMS, JavaScript application architecture,
build system, component library, dependency stack, or other technical
complexity unless the project develops a concrete requirement that
justifies it.

Prefer the simplest implementation that preserves accessibility,
responsiveness, maintainability, performance, semantic HTML, and visual
fidelity.

Technical sophistication is not itself a project goal.

## 19. Implementation workflow

For significant changes:

**inspect → propose → implement locally → review → approve →
commit/publish**

Do not make broad visual or architectural changes without first
identifying what problem they solve.

When a requested visual adjustment can be achieved through a small CSS
or asset-placement change, prefer that over rebuilding the page.

When implementation differs visibly from an approved concept, determine
whether the problem comes from artwork, crop, scale, positioning,
typography, spacing, responsive behavior, CSS implementation, asset
conversion, or browser rendering before changing the underlying design.

# CURRENT APPROVED DIRECTION

## 20. Homepage

The homepage establishes the site's principal visual language.

Its signature artwork uses a large, translucent watercolor octopus
integrated with biological/scientific motifs and substantial negative
space.

The octopus artwork should be treated as **signature art**, not as a
template requiring similar imagery everywhere.

Current homepage messaging centers on:

**Exploring the science of wellness**

with research-focused supporting language concerning longevity and
personalized medicine.

The current homepage should be treated as an evolving but intentional
design, not as a generic starting template.

## 21. Systems Medicine

"Systems Medicine" is the current research-area name encompassing areas
such as genomics, multiomics, systems biology, network medicine, and
data-driven approaches to individualized biology.

Current working introduction:

> Genomics and other omic technologies provide increasingly detailed
> views of individual biology. Systems approaches integrate these data
> with environmental and behavioral factors to understand health as a
> dynamic network of interactions---and to explore more personalized
> approaches to prevention and treatment.

The approved conceptual artwork uses **DNA dissolving into a modular
molecular-interaction/protein-network structure**.

The network should dominate the scientific morphology; DNA is
subordinate.

The network should exhibit characteristics of real biological-network
visualization: irregular topology, modules/clusters, hubs, variable node
prominence, bridging interactions, and sparse peripheral regions.

It remains conceptual artwork and must not imply that it represents
measured biological data.

The Systems Medicine page currently contains placeholder Research Review
entries. They are not real editorial content.

# DECISION HIERARCHY

## 22. When making new design decisions

Evaluate proposed changes in this order:

**1. Does it strengthen the intellectual purpose of the site?**

**2. Does it belong to the established visual language?**

**3. Does it improve hierarchy or comprehension?**

**4. Is it scientifically meaningful rather than merely decorative?**

**5. Does it preserve sufficient negative space?**

**6. Is it simpler than an equally effective alternative?**

Do not optimize individual sections in isolation at the expense of the
site's overall rhythm and identity.

## 23. Preserve coherence, not sameness

The goal is not to make every page look alike.

The goal is for every page to feel as though it belongs to the **same
intellectual and visual world**.

Variation is desirable when driven by subject matter. Repetition is
desirable when it establishes useful structure. Neither should become
mechanical.
